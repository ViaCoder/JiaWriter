# Sentence length

Read when the check reports sentence length, 、 density or requirement words off their range. Official sentences get their length from parallel elements, not from rhetoric.

## Which side it is off

- Research reports, speeches, experience materials and summaries tend to have too little parallel detail: add it.
- Opinions and plans tend to have too much: cut it.

## Too little: four ways to add

Two or three are often combined in one sentence. What is added is information; a paragraph added for length only waters the text down.

1. Parallel matters: name the several things instead of "this matter", each with what can be measured.
2. Parallel actors and duties: say which level or body takes which share.
3. Parallel conditions: list the scope, use, time limit and who is responsible, item by item.
4. A closing clause that states the effect or the floor the action must hold.

Few requirement words mean the actions are not written out: make the action concrete first, then 要 has something to govern. A summary of finished work takes its requirement words from a closing 确保, not from 要.

## Too much: how to cut

- Keep the three items that best define the scope; move the rest later or drop them.
- Use a clause instead of a long 、 list: a sentence with two clauses reads clearer than five items in a row, even when longer.
- When mandatory words are over their count, replace a prohibition with a decision or a correction, which keeps the force (一律取消 for 不得下派, 要一并纠正 for 不得违规使用).

## Where not to add

- Quotations of what people said: they are short and stay so.
- Scenes: short sentences and plain description; parallel lists kill the picture.
- The binding clauses of normative documents: each is already a requirement.
- A short sentence that is a quotation, a scene or a rhetorical question stays short.
