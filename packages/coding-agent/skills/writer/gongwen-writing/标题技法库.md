# Headings

Patterns of real official headings.

## By genre

| Heading | First choice | Also | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statutory document title | issuing body + matter + genre | — | any device |
| Title of a report or summary | state the matter (most use no device) | a quoted concept, a number that sums up | figures of speech, conditions |
| Level-1 heading, analytical genres | an action, then its purpose, in two clauses | a quoted concept, a number that sums up | figures of speech, contrasts |
| Level-1 heading, plans and opinions | 4–8 characters, stated plainly | — | any device |
| Speech title | parallel halves or three parallel parts | a figure of speech, a number | — |

## Devices

- Two clauses: the first an action, the second its purpose.
- A quoted concept: an everyday word in quotation marks as the object of analysis; two may be set against each other.
- A number that sums up the practices; one group of headings uses one number pattern.
- Three parallel parts: for titles and long headings only. Parallel halves: the same structure, lengths within two characters.
- Figures of speech only in titles and speech headings, almost never in level-1 headings; conditional chains (方能, 才能) and A-versus-B contrasts belong to commentary, not to official headings.

## A group of headings

One group uses one device throughout (all numbers, all questions, or all verb-object), with lengths within three characters of each other.
